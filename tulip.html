Why it exists

Most travel apps treat a trip like a receipt: a list of bookings you look at once and forget. We wanted the opposite. Tulip puts everywhere you've been and everywhere you want to go on one globe, so your travel feels like yours.

Brenden and I started it in May 2026. It's invite-only for now while we get it ready for everyone.

What's in it

  • Miles

    Tell Miles what kind of trip you want. He plans every day and pins it to your map, and nothing is saved until you say so.

  • Bookings inbox

    Forward a confirmation email and the flight, stay or booking lands in the right trip, on the right day.

  • Trips

    Plan with friends day by day. Leave notes, stick reminders and see who's in.

  • Passport

    Every country you've visited lights up, and every trip leaves a stamp. Share it or keep it to yourself.

  • iPhone

    The whole globe in your pocket, with today's plan on your lock screen. Coming soon.

Pins
Save a café or a whole country. Places you want to go and places you've been each get their own colour.
Flights
Add a flight and watch it cross the globe, with the landing time already in local time.
Nearby
Open any pin to see the food, stays, sights and transit around it.

How it's built

The website is built with Next.js and React around a 3D Mapbox globe. Behind it is a Python server with FastAPI and a Postgres database on Supabase, which also handles sign-in. The iPhone app is written in SwiftUI and talks to the same server.

Hard parts

  1. A globe that stays smooth.

    Pins, trips, flights and search results all draw on one 3D globe at the same time, and it still has to feel quick on a laptop.

  2. Time zones everywhere.

    A flight leaves in one time zone and lands in another. Every time on screen has to be in the right one.

  3. From email to itinerary.

    Booking emails all look different. Turning one into the right flight or stay, on the right day of the right trip, is harder than it sounds.
